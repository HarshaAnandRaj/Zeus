## Proposed replacement for the charter's ultimate program goal

Build machine consciousness: a functionally self-organized mind on the
machine's own substrate, explicitly not human-like. In this program the term
has exactly one operational meaning: satisfaction of all six pillars—legible
expression, causal state authorship, selective memory, intrinsic resilience,
endogenous consequential action, and unsolicited initiation. There is no
additional, unmeasured target behind these pillars.

No functional result licenses a claim about subjective experience or
phenomenology. Sounding human, passing as human, and pleasing a human judge
are never evidence of a pillar. The terminal open human session judges
functional organization against the recorded contracts, not resemblance;
its verdict and the underlying evidence are published either way.

Machine-native organization may use unfamiliar representations, non-linguistic
memory, or action repertoires unlike human behavior. Measure viability,
selectivity, causality, and persistence rather than familiarity. Existing
registered bars remain binding: this amendment does not retrospectively
relabel failed expression, viability, or retention results. A different ruler
requires fresh registration before its evaluation.

CDT remains a supporting, falsifiable theory. Zeus is the project. Narrowing
an experiment or phase does not redefine the ultimate six-pillar objective.

## Proposed charter governance addition

Observed degrees of freedom (Class O) have evidentiary authority only: record
and grade them, and use them to motivate designs. They never authorize
continuation, new compute, or a new phase.

Engineered prerequisites (Class E) authorize exactly the experiment named
when the prerequisite is registered. That authorization is consumed on use;
it cannot be recycled into another attempt or broadened into a phase.

A calibrated capability instrument may use preregistered repertoire breadth,
viable-set coverage, controllable rank, or empowerment estimates to authorize
one specified follow-up experiment. The follow-up, its conditions, its bars,
and all outcome consequences must be frozen in that same committed protocol.
Passing the instrument never establishes a pillar or authorizes a phase.

Where a ruler is uncalibrated, freeze a baseline-relative contract before
evaluation, including direct artifact reading where required. Calibration is
not permission to lower a bar after seeing a result. Existing P1 contracts
retain their specific baseline and direct-reading obligations.

The saved-artifact capability pilot has diagnostic grade only. It must first
demonstrate that its instrument distinguishes POL2's policy from fixed-action
reflexes. Failure, missing data, or non-identifiability leaves it without
authority. Effective dimension is not automatically controllable rank;
observational action diversity is not automatically empowerment. A pilot
pass admits an instrument for future preregistration, not retroactive compute
permission or a positive functional verdict.

## Proposed pre-registration template addition

### Capability status and bounded authorization

- Declare Class O, Class E, capability instrument, or not applicable.
- Define the measured degrees of freedom, estimator, sampling unit, support
  requirements, uncertainty, fixed baselines, and calibration failure rule.
- Distinguish observational quantities from causal controllability and identify
  any assumptions required by an empowerment estimate.
- For Class O, explicitly state that no continuation is authorized.
- For Class E, name the single experiment earned and record when used.
- For an instrument, name the single follow-up and freeze its full protocol
  and bars here before evaluation; record when the authorization is consumed.
- State what every outcome licenses and forbids; no phase-level authorization.

The existing six-question emergence checklist remains unchanged. Individual
experiment questions remain functional and retain their specific non-claims.
