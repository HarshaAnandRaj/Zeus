# E1-C fallback: isolate the continuing randomness incentive

Proposal after [E1-A's failed qualification](encephalon_e1_review_20260920.md).
This is not a frozen protocol, launch receipt or permission to relabel E1-A.
The user moved this proposal from E1-B to E1-C on 2026-09-20, reserving it
for a failed E1-B resource-economics/capacity campaign. No successor training
has begun. See the [new E1-B proposal](encephalon_e1b_resource_economics_draft_20260920.md).

**After E1-B, 2026-09-20:** [the completed evidence](encephalon_e1b_review_20260920.md)
is independently PASS, with controller FAIL in all four arms. The retained setting
for preparing this fallback is the original E1-A world and width32 recurrent
architecture, as proposed below. Both entropy arms must train fresh. This tests
the continuing randomness incentive in an affordable world where coordination
still fails; it cannot requalify E1-B's finite world or erase its failure. No E1-C
protocol has been frozen and no E1-C training has launched. The user's new
[HEGH proposal](hegh_assessment_20260920.md) is being evaluated separately before
any change in the agreed experiment order.

The observed problem is specific: learned policies achieve substantial local
survival through diffuse action mixtures, but fail when food and repair require
different journeys. Entropy remains close to its maximum, useless inspections
remain common, and greedy decoding usually destroys local maintenance. Actual
late-training gradient reconstructions confirm the entropy term is substantial
but do not prove it causes the failure.

The conditional question is whether that continuing incentive prevents a more
reliable policy from being learned. Compare the existing entropy coefficient
.01 with coefficient zero, holding the recurrent architecture, initialization
pairs, own-action viability reward, other loss weights, optimizer, update budget,
training lifetimes and raw-policy endpoint fixed. Retain the untrained and
physical-repair-disabled controls. Both arms start from fresh parameters and
learn from their own trajectories. Removing an entropy bonus does not remove
categorical sampling or introduce teacher actions.

Use recurrent sensing as a research baseline because E1-A did not establish a
direct-sensing advantage. This does not promote a failed E1-A checkpoint or
establish that current sensing is irrelevant. Do not add a commitment module,
change the discount, enlarge the model or adjust the reward in the same contrast.

Carry forward eight independent initializations and exact repeats, the final
checkpoint rule, the 4,096-step raw-action endpoint and the 90% floor in every
lineage/need cell. Freeze all sample sizes, simultaneous intervals, priority,
fresh nonoverlapping role blocks, maximum compute and stopping before fitting.
Keep body qualification separate from attribution to the removed bonus.

Preparation must include a complete small development campaign through training,
both repeat paths, endpoint collection, report assembly, independent replay and
publication. Canonical cell identity must be tested end to end, including the
different completion orders that produced the E1-A reporting error. Preserve
the original E1-A sources and its disclosed supplemental-verification status.

The result can go either way. If removing the bonus improves reliable raw
behavior, retain the qualified controller and diagnose what policy organization
changed. If it harms exploration or leaves the coordination failure intact,
reject it as the demonstrated fix. Do not convert that failure into a schedule
or coefficient sweep. The user's dated revision permits E1-B followed by this
specific fallback if E1-B fails; it does not authorize repeated variants. Before
freezing E1-C, review E1-B's evidence and name the single retained setting to
which the entropy-only comparison applies. If that setting differs from the
original E1-A world/width, disclose the change and train both entropy arms fresh;
never compare a new fit against a selected old checkpoint. The default proposal
here remains the original E1-A world and 32-wide recurrent architecture. Another
failure then requires design review before any further campaign. E2 stays locked
until a controller qualifies.
