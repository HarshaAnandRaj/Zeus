Task: Develop Temporal HEGH into a preregisterable experiment ready for Codex

We have completed HEGH-0 and a subsequent structural audit. I now want you to stop debating the general idea and turn the surviving hypothesis into a rigorous, minimal experiment specification that can be handed directly to Codex for implementation.

Your job is scientific design, not coding. Codex will implement what you specify. Do not leave major scientific choices for Codex to infer.

⸻

1. Fixed prior result: HEGH-0 is closed

Do not reinterpret, repair, reopen, or retroactively change HEGH-0.

HEGH-0 was an engineered one-decision accessibility assay with equal-mean Wide and Narrow transition-price constructions.

Primary price mean:

> \mu_C=1.
>

At reserve B=1.1:

> P(\text{safe requested access})_W=3565/4096=87.04\%
>

versus

> P(\text{safe requested access})_N=4096/4096=100\%.
>

At reserve B=0.9:

> P(\text{access})_W=555/4096=13.55\%
>

versus

> P(\text{access})_N=0.
>

Narrow therefore removed costly upper-tail transitions near an above-mean affordability boundary, but also removed the cheap lower tail below that boundary.

This reversal is an empirical property of the actual HEGH-0 engineered distributions, not a universal theorem about variance, mean-preserving spreads, risk sensitivity, or all B<\mu_C and B>\mu_C.

HEGH-0 also established that concentration did not generically make first departure easier.

There were no neural fits and no natural geometry→operational-cost bridge in HEGH-0.

⸻

2. Why a new experiment is needed

HEGH-0 used:

> B=\text{externally fixed starting reserve}
>

a static candidate/resource structure, one decision, and episode termination after that decision.

A continuing agent instead obeys something like

> B_{t+1}=B_t-C_t+R_t-M_t,
>

while the opportunity state can itself evolve:

> \mathcal R_t\rightarrow\mathcal R_{t+1}.
>

Resources can appear, disappear, deplete, replenish, or change usefulness.

Current need state N_t may determine which available resource is consequential.

If the controller contains memory, a sufficient internal state H_t may also matter.

The conceptual full state is therefore approximately

> X_t=(B_t,\mathcal R_t,N_t,H_t),
>

with components removable only if the experimental design makes them irrelevant.

We have already rejected the following stronger claims:

* greater variance universally gives Wide an advantage below the mean;
* Narrow universally reduces entry into low-budget states;
* Wide universally improves recovery from low-budget states;
* stationary occupation P(B_t<\mu_C) determines viability;
* a budget-only Markov kernel is automatically sufficient;
* matching a coupled system’s aggregate survival curve with a memoryless model proves temporal coupling causally irrelevant.

Do not restore any of these claims downstream.

⸻

3. Surviving scientific hypothesis

Develop and sharpen the following working hypothesis:

> \boxed{
> \text{HEGH's local transition-cost structure interacts with endogenous reserve dynamics and history-dependent opportunity dynamics, such that trajectory-level viability cannot necessarily be inferred from the closed one-step HEGH-0 accessibility result.}
> }
>

A stronger operational version worth evaluating is:

> \boxed{
> \text{Holding relevant one-step cost/resource marginals fixed, changing the temporal organization of the opportunity process can change the trajectory-level effect of Wide versus Narrow HEGH cost structure.}
> }
>

This does not predict:

> Wide>Narrow
>

or

> Narrow>Wide.
>

The sign is deliberately unknown.

Determine whether this should be one hypothesis or decomposed into:

> H_{\text{temporal}}
>

— temporal organization affects continuing viability at all —

and

> H_{\text{interaction}}
>

— temporal organization specifically modifies the effect of HEGH Wide/Narrow cost structure.

I suspect the second is the scientifically more HEGH-specific claim, but audit this rather than simply accepting it.

⸻

4. Null hypothesis

Construct an actual falsifiable/equivalence null.

A candidate is:

> H_0:
>

once relevant one-step cost, access, resource-value, and total-supply statistics are matched, changing temporal organization does not consequentially alter the Wide-vs-Narrow trajectory dynamics beyond a preregistered equivalence margin.

Do not use simple failure to reach significance as evidence for H_0.

Specify what equivalence test or preregistered effect bound would be required to conclude that temporal organization is practically irrelevant in this assay.

5. Critical issue: do NOT assume B=\mu_C is the true viability boundary

We previously used B<1 and B>1 because HEGH-0’s engineered price mean was 1.

In a dynamic system containing R_t and M_t, there is no guarantee that

> B=\mu_C
>

is the meaningful safe/vulnerable boundary.

Audit this explicitly.

Prefer either:

* analysis continuously over B_t, or
* a viability boundary defined independently from the Wide/Narrow result, such as a preregistered committor/ruin-risk criterion.

Do not choose a threshold because it makes the HEGH effect easy to see.

If a threshold is necessary, derive it before looking at Wide/Narrow outcomes.

6. Experimental object

Design the smallest dynamic ecology capable of testing the temporal hypothesis.

It must have:

> B_{t+1}=B_t-C_t+R_t-M_t
>

and a genuinely changing opportunity state.

At minimum, some resources must be consumable/depletable and subsequently replenishable or replaced, so that an action at time t can alter opportunities available at t+k.

Avoid decorative complexity.

We do not need a realistic ecosystem.

We need the smallest system in which:

> \text{current access}
> \rightarrow
> \text{resource use}
> \rightarrow
> \text{future opportunity state}
> \rightarrow
> \text{future access}
>

genuinely forms a loop.

Determine whether changing needs N_t are necessary for this first temporal assay. If they are not required to identify the mechanism, freeze them and defer them.

Likewise determine whether a recurrent neural controller H_t is necessary.

My preference is to begin with the simplest fixed or explicitly specified policy if that isolates the mechanism better, then test a learned controller later.

Do not add a neural network merely because Zeus ultimately uses one.

7. Wide/Narrow manipulation

Reuse the actual HEGH-0 Wide/Narrow construction as faithfully as possible.

The manipulation should remain:

8. Paired ecology requirement

Wide and Narrow should encounter matched exogenous worlds.

For each seed/map, pre-generate the same:

> \text{resource appearance schedule},
>

> \text{resource values},
>

> \text{renewal events},
>

> \text{maintenance process},
>

and all other exogenous randomness.

Then allow their realized opportunity states to diverge endogenously because their actions consume/deplete resources differently.

Thus:

> \mathcal R^{W}_0=\mathcal R^{N}_0
>

but potentially

> \mathcal R^{W}_t\neq\mathcal R^{N}_t
>

later.

This divergence is a feature, not a confound, provided the external schedule is paired.

Specify exactly which randomness must be common-random-number paired and which randomness must remain condition-specific.

9. Temporal-organization intervention

I want the design to test temporal coupling causally rather than merely fit a richer model afterward.

Consider a factorial manipulation:

> \text{Cost structure}\in\{Wide,Narrow\}
>

crossed with

> \text{Temporal organization}\in\{Structured,Temporal\ Null\}.
>

The Structured and Temporal-Null environments should preserve as many static/one-step quantities as possible while differing in temporal organization.

Candidate possibilities include preserving:

* total resource supply;
* resource-value multiset;
* marginal availability;
* mean replenishment;
* starting state;
* spatial candidate set;
* maintenance;
* cost distribution;

while changing only things such as temporal autocorrelation, replenishment ordering, burstiness, persistence, or event order.

One possible null is temporal permutation of an otherwise identical resource-event sequence.

Another is two finite-state availability processes with identical stationary marginals but different persistence/autocorrelation.

Another is matched total replenishment delivered steadily versus in temporally clustered bursts.

Evaluate these and choose the cleanest minimal intervention.

Be careful: if temporal manipulation changes one-step availability or average reward, it does not isolate temporal organization.

State exactly what is preserved and what changes.

11. Metrics

Do not use survival time alone.

Develop the smallest measurement set capable of distinguishing different trajectory mechanisms.

Candidate quantities include:

> S(t)=P(\tau_{\rm ruin}>t),
>

the full time-to-ruin/first-passage distribution,

transition into a preregistered vulnerable region,

depth of budget excursions,

repeated entries,

and recovery defined as a first-passage/committor quantity:

> P(\tau_{\rm safe}<\tau_{\rm ruin}\mid X_t\in V).
>

Recovery should not necessarily mean “cross the threshold in one step.”

Measure recovery-time distributions if useful.

Also consider:

> \Delta B_t=-C_t+R_t-M_t,
>

resource depletion state at vulnerable entry,

useful opportunities remaining,

successful acquisitions,

and resource-state-conditioned hazard.

But avoid an enormous metric zoo.

Separate:

primary outcome(s),

mechanism diagnostics,

and exploratory diagnostics.

12. Accessibility is not recovery

Preserve these distinctions explicitly:

> \text{resource accessible}
> \neq
> \text{resource useful},
>

> \text{resource useful}
> \neq
> \text{net-positive recovery},
>

and

> \text{net-positive step}
> \neq
> \text{long-term viability}.
>

If a resource costs C_t, provides R_t, and maintenance costs M_t, the relevant budget change is

> R_t-C_t-M_t.
>

Do not label a cheap-tail transition a “rescue” until the trajectory actually satisfies a preregistered recovery criterion.

13. Causal versus predictive sufficiency

Therefore:

> S_{\rm reduced}(t)\approx S_{\rm coupled}(t)
>

is not by itself falsification.

If you use reduced models, use them as diagnostics.

A stronger causal test should rely on temporal interventions while preserving relevant marginals.

Specify what result would count as:

> \text{failure to detect temporal coupling},
>

versus

> \text{positive evidence of practical equivalence},
>

versus

> \text{positive evidence that temporal organization changes HEGH-mediated viability}.
>
